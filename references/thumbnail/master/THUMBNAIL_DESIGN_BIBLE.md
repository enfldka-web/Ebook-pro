# Atlas Thumbnail Design Bible

Status: permanent project knowledge for Atlas V3 Phase 2 (Thumbnail Engine only — not used for Sales Page).
Source: Atlas V3 Master Thumbnail Reference (4 themes × 5 examples each), studied and generalized into reusable
commercial design rules. No branding, wording, exact colors, layouts, or illustrations from the source images are
reproduced here — only the underlying commercial-design logic.

This document is the standing evaluation baseline: every real generated Atlas thumbnail is judged against the
rules below, per theme.

---

## 0. Cross-theme findings (apply before reading the per-theme rules)

- **Photographic AI-generated scenes are the exception, not the default.** Only one of the four themes
  (Publisher Premium) is built around a photographed/rendered product mockup. The other three lean on flat or
  duotone backgrounds, bold typography, and a single simple icon/pictogram — closer to a marketing graphic than
  a photo. Any theme that currently asks the AI image provider for a full photographic "scene" where the
  reference shows flat/graphic treatment is over-relying on photography.
- **Numbers and keywords are literal, styled text — not a shape hidden inside a photo.** Every reference example
  emphasizes exactly one number or keyword phrase through color/weight contrast in the actual headline
  typography. That is Overlay/typography work, not something the image-generation prompt needs to encode as an
  abstract visual motif.
- **One accent color per thumbnail, not a full palette.** Each example commits to a single dominant brand/accent
  color plus a neutral ground. Multiple competing saturated colors in one frame reads as unfinished, not premium.
- **One focal element per frame.** Every example has exactly one thing the eye lands on first (a product mockup,
  an icon, or the headline itself) — never two competing focal points.
- **Small, single trust/authority cue, never decorative clutter.** Badges/seals appear at most once per
  thumbnail, are small, and sit in a corner — never centered, never large, never more than one per frame.

## 1. Publisher Premium

**Commercial category:** premium nonfiction / business book, positioned like a real published title.

- **Composition:** frame splits into two clear zones — a flat-color text zone and a product-mockup zone. Not a
  full-bleed photo with text laid on top.
- **Product presentation:** the book/tablet mockup is treated like a catalog product photo — softly lit, centered
  in its zone, on a neutral surface, at most one small lifestyle prop (never more than one, never competing for
  attention with the product itself).
- **Typography hierarchy:** one bold headline (max 2 lines) + one small subtitle line beneath it. No third text
  tier.
- **Color strategy:** exactly one flat brand color per piece (the text-zone background), with the product-zone
  kept neutral/soft so the mockup reads clearly against it.
- **Authority cue:** at most one small circular/seal-style badge, corner-placed, never dominant.
- **Emotional register:** calm, restrained, confident — the opposite of urgency or loud color.
- **Marketplace clickability check:** at 163×122, the split itself (flat color block vs. product photo) should
  still read as two zones — if it collapses into visual mush at small size, the panel contrast is too low.

## 2. Problem Solver

**Commercial category:** how-to / practical guide, positioned as a solution to a specific frustration.

- **Composition:** white or very light ground, headline top-left to top-center, one large flat-color icon/graphic
  bottom-right or right-aligned. Reading order is strictly: headline → highlighted number/keyword → icon →
  supporting checklist/steps.
- **Product presentation:** no literal product mockup required — the icon *is* the product presentation (it
  represents the outcome, not the deliverable object).
- **Typography hierarchy:** headline in two visual weights — most of the sentence in near-black, the
  number/keyword phrase in one accent color. Optional short sub-line beneath in lighter weight.
- **Proof/benefit presentation:** benefits render as a short checklist (✔ + 2-4 word phrases) or a literal
  numbered step sequence (1 → 2 → 3), never as a paragraph.
- **Visual hook:** the graphic must be identifiable in silhouette alone (arrow+growth, target, magnifying
  glass+chart) — if it needs close inspection to understand, it fails the "3-second read" bar.
- **Color strategy:** one accent color drives both the icon and the highlighted keyword — they should feel like
  one system, not two unrelated color choices.

## 3. Bestseller Editorial

**Commercial category:** insight/mindset/strategy book positioned like a bestselling nonfiction cover.

- **Composition:** typography-dominant — the headline itself should occupy roughly 60-70% of the frame's visual
  weight. Everything else is secondary.
- **Product presentation:** no literal product mockup. Instead, one small symbolic graphic (an outline/silhouette
  motif that echoes the book's idea) sits behind or beside the type, always subordinate to the typography.
  Photographic backgrounds are permitted here but must be desaturated/moody, never bright or lifestyle-toned —
  the type must still dominate over the photo.
- **Typography scale:** largest headline scale of all four themes. Line breaks should land on natural phrase
  boundaries, not mid-clause.
- **Authority cue:** one small "bestseller/proof" badge in a corner — this is the one theme where a numeric proof
  badge (copies sold, ranking) is appropriate, but only ever one, small, and only when the number is real.
- **Emotional register:** serious, confident, literary — avoid playful color or rounded/friendly iconography.
- **Color strategy:** single flat or moody-gradient background; gold/metallic-feeling accents read as premium
  when used as a single thin line or ring, not filled shapes.

## 4. Marketplace Impact

**Commercial category:** digital product / course listing optimized for a crowded marketplace grid.

- **Composition:** flat or diagonal two-color split background; one large, simple, logo/app-icon-style graphic
  occupies roughly a third of the frame; headline occupies the rest at maximum boldness.
- **Product presentation:** the "product" is represented by a single iconic graphic standing in for the
  tool/outcome (an app-style badge, a simple line-icon of a device/interface) — never a busy illustrated scene.
- **Typography hierarchy:** the largest, boldest text-to-frame ratio of the four themes; one keyword/number
  always carries the single accent color, the rest of the headline stays high-contrast neutral (black/white).
- **Contrast strategy:** always maximum background-to-text contrast — this theme has zero tolerance for
  low-contrast pairings, because it's judged at the smallest real display size (a marketplace grid thumbnail).
- **Marketplace clickability check:** this is the theme to stress-test hardest at 163×122 — if the icon and
  keyword aren't both instantly legible at that size, the design has failed regardless of how it looks full-size.
- **Emotional register:** energetic, direct, conversion-first — the most "advertisement-like" of the four themes,
  and that is correct for this theme, not a flaw.

## 5. Reusable rules that apply across all four themes

- **Reading order is always: dominant visual/typography → highlighted number or keyword → supporting
  graphic/product → secondary text.** No theme buries the headline beneath a graphic.
- **Whitespace is a design choice, not empty space.** Every reference example leaves deliberate breathing room
  around its focal element — density is never used to signal "more value."
- **Korean headline line breaks must land on phrase boundaries**, never splitting a noun+particle or a
  number+unit across lines.
- **CTA presence is theme-dependent, not universal** — Publisher Premium and Bestseller Editorial often omit an
  explicit CTA (the quiet/authority tone carries it); Problem Solver and Marketplace Impact use one short,
  action-verb CTA when present.
- **Never fabricate proof.** Badges, "bestseller," ranking, or sold-count cues only ever appear when backed by a
  real signal — an empty corner is always preferable to an invented number.
