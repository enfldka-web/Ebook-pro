## 1. Visual Language (Unified)

Six qualities recurred across both a single-glance thumbnail reference set and a sustained-scroll sales-page reference set:

- **Text is the hero, imagery is support.** Headlines and section labels consistently carry more visual weight than photography, which is pushed back (desaturated, cropped, textural) rather than left to compete.
- **One dominant hue, sustained.** Confirmed at two scales: once per thumbnail image, and — more strongly — across an entire multi-section sales page. Color discipline is a campaign-level decision every asset in a product's visual set must inherit.
- **Three-tier hierarchy, repeating.** A small credibility/label tier, a dominant message tier, and a small supporting tier — present in a single thumbnail frame and repeating at every section boundary of a sales page.
- **Restraint reads as premium; density and urgency read as cheap.**
- **A visual element must encode meaning, never decorate emptily.**
- **Credibility is quiet; the hook is loud.**

## 2. Thumbnail Philosophy

The thumbnail's job is won or lost in under a second. A short, emotionally specific fragment carries the entire first impression. The supporting visual should encode the headline's literal meaning. Photography of people is pushed back into a textural/secondary role. Extreme size contrast between the credibility line and the headline is the mechanism that makes hierarchy legible without reading a single word. A textural typographic effect (not photographic ornamentation) is the primary carrier of "crafted" or "premium" feeling.

## 3. Sales Page Philosophy

One dominant hue is sustained across the entire page; pacing is created through tone shifts, never hard dividing lines. The persuasion arc that recurred is **Hook → Pain → Mechanism → Proof → Usage → Audience Confirmation → Closing Facts** — notably not the generic Problem→Agitation→Solution→Benefits→Proof→Offer→FAQ→CTA template. Mechanism earns its own stage rather than being folded into Solution, and Audience Confirmation lands late, functioning as reassurance after proof rather than an early filter. Desire is built through specificity rather than urgency or scarcity. No reference embedded its own CTA button or FAQ block inside the artifact — both belong to the surrounding marketplace platform.

## 4. Commercial Psychology (Merged)

```
Thumbnail: Curiosity Gap (the hook that earns the click)
                    ↓
Sales Page Hero: Confirmation (same campaign mood)
                    ↓
Pain (named specifically)
                    ↓
Mechanism (why/how the solution actually works)
                    ↓
Proof (numbers, credibility signals, review evidence)
                    ↓
Usage (what using this actually looks like)
                    ↓
Audience Confirmation (reassurance, not a filter)
                    ↓
Closing Facts (hand-off to the external purchase mechanism)
```

The thumbnail's curiosity gap and the sales page's Hero must be read by the buyer as one continuous thought, not two separate persuasion attempts.

## 5. Conversion Design — The Full Buyer Journey

Color, mood, and campaign concept must be decided once, upstream of both artifact types, and inherited by each — not independently re-decided per surface. Quality evaluation of a thumbnail in isolation, or a sales page Hero in isolation, is incomplete — the real test is whether the two, viewed in sequence, read as one uninterrupted argument.

## 6. Design Patterns — Confidence Ranking

**High confidence:** one dominant hue per campaign; three-tier size hierarchy; restraint reads as premium; credibility signals always smaller/quieter than the emotional hook; visual elements must encode meaning.

**Medium confidence:** Mechanism as its own persuasion stage; Audience Confirmation placed late; absence of embedded CTA/FAQ; textural typographic effects as the primary premium carrier; motif-and-headline fusion.

**Low confidence / needs more references:** whitespace density scaling with brand tone; literal numbered step blocks as a universal format; authority/certification signals as category-gated.

## 7. Component Library

**Thumbnail-surface components:** Concept Card, Textural Headline Treatment, Overlay Copy Block.

**Sales-page-surface components:** Hero Block, Pain Callout Block, Mechanism/Science Block, Proof Block, Testimonial Block, Usage/Step Block, Audience Confirmation Block, Closing Facts Block.

**Shared/cross-cutting components:** Section Tone-Shift mechanism, Campaign Color Token.

## 8. Future Prompt Implications

Creative Direction should plan a thumbnail's motif and headline as one joint decision. Sales-page scene planning should treat Mechanism and Proof as two distinct planning stages. Audience-confirmation copy should be planned as a late-sequence element by default. Campaign-level color/mood should be decided once, upstream of both thumbnail and sales-page Creative Direction.

## 9. Future AI Generation Rules

- **Color should be a campaign-level constraint, not a per-asset one.**
- **No generation scene should assume it needs to render a CTA button or FAQ content.**
- **A thumbnail and its campaign's sales-page Hero should be validated together, not independently.**
- **Mechanism and Proof should be distinguishable, separately-labeled planning fields.**
